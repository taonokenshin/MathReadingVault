# Evidence and qualifications

The public release separates three kinds of evidence. Software tests establish observed behavior on their fixtures. Source-linked mathematical notes preserve the reading project's arguments and qualifications. Artifact manifests establish file identity and completeness within a stated selection. None of these alone establishes mathematical correctness, novelty, teaching effectiveness or general reliability.

The delimiter checker is a heuristic, not a full TeX parser or rendering test. A source hash is provenance, not proof certification. The source-window cache uses path/size/mtime and requires local Poppler; it is not content-authenticated archiving. The controller cannot measure mathematical attention or decide whether a proof is valid.

## Historical mathematical labels

Reader-reconstructed, cited, conditional, attempted, source-caveat, closed-pass1 and closed-reviewed retain their recorded scopes. Dates and correction links matter. An old open label may be superseded; a later local closure does not prove a broader theorem excluded by its hypotheses. Internal open-hard labels do not mean the literature has no solution. Reader corrections are not publisher-approved errata.

Publication does not rerun a mathematical proof audit. The learner may study existing arguments and choose suitable exercises without recreating the entire historical verification process. Source books, published results and external references keep their authorship and rights.
