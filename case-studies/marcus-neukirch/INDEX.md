# Marcus–Neukirch: twelve mathematical case studies

## Reader preface

These English case studies show how a sustained, user-directed and Codex-assisted reading project turns difficult source material into explanations, supplementary derivations, convention comparisons, counterexamples, and durable continuation records. Read either textbook route in its own order; cross-book comparisons are support, not a merged chapter sequence or an imposed learning plan.

This public selection comes from the preserved study edition dated **20 October 2026**. That historical edition contains **1,152 mathematical article files** and **1,124 normalized session-record files**. Those are archive inventory counts, not distinct theorems, certified proofs, successful scheduled occurrences, users, or evidence of adoption. The twelve pages here are a curated sample. A separate archive, if supplied, has its own scope and provenance.

The mathematics is classical. The project’s contribution is reader exposition, explicit prerequisites and dependencies, expanded reasoning, diagnostic examples, correction history, and a reproducible way to preserve study progress. “Supplementary argument” does not assert research novelty. Historical `closed-pass1` and `closed-reviewed` labels are attributed to the source project; this public edition performs **no new proof re-verification** and upgrades no label.

## Marcus route

Prepare elementary algebra and linear algebra before the Chapter 2 articles. The Chapter 7 and 8 cases require the intervening textbook material and the explicit analytic or class-field inputs identified in their prefaces.

- [Embeddings, Trace, and Norm](marcus/02-embeddings-trace-norm.md) — Marcus Chapter 2; `MA0060`. Connect conjugates, linear algebra, relative trace/norm, and a higher-degree counterexample.
- [Deriving Two Families of Trinomial Discriminants](marcus/02-trinomial-discriminants.md) — Marcus Chapter 2; `MA0066`. Show extended algebraic computation rather than a final discriminant formula alone.
- [From Ideal Counting to Dedekind Zeta](marcus/07-ideal-counting-to-zeta.md) — Marcus Chapter 7; `MA0140`. Explain Abel summation, convergence, continuation, the Euler product, and the role of the counting error.
- [Why Class Fields Enter Prime Distribution](marcus/08-prime-distribution-and-class-fields.md) — Marcus Chapter 8; `MA0170`. Show how distribution, character nonvanishing, and principal primes meet in the Hilbert class field.

## Neukirch route

The selected pages follow Chapters I–VII. The space between selected cases is intentional: this is a portfolio of interfaces and proof techniques, not a replacement for the complete textbook. Individual prefaces identify prerequisites rather than silently assuming that the preceding selected page is sufficient.

- [Minkowski Space and Two Measure Conventions](neukirch/01-minkowski-measure-comparison.md) — Neukirch I §5; `NA0044`. Explain the real-coordinate metric and reconcile the complex-place factor between the two textbooks.
- [Hensel Lifting as Iterative Factor Correction](neukirch/02-hensel-factorization.md) — Neukirch II §4; `NA0087`. Make the linearized correction, shrinking error, degree control, and completeness step visible.
- [From Trace Duality to the Different](neukirch/03-trace-dual-and-different.md) — Neukirch III §2; `NA0141`. Connect module duality, fractional ideals, localization, completion, and later ramification calculations.
- [Three Mechanisms Behind Hilbert 90](neukirch/04-three-hilbert-90-mechanisms.md) — Neukirch IV §3; `NA0559`. Present multiplicative weighted sums, additive trace-one integration, and Witt filtered lifting with failure boundaries.
- [Tame Hilbert Symbols: Valuation, Units, and Frobenius](neukirch/05-tame-hilbert-pairing.md) — Neukirch V §3; `NA0242`. Track how bilinearity, residue units, roots of unity, and Frobenius normalization determine the formula.
- [Why Norm-One Idele Classes Are Compact](neukirch/06-norm-one-idele-classes.md) — Neukirch VI §1; `NA0290`. Display two complementary routes and clarify the kernel, quotient, compact-extension, and S-unit interfaces.
- [A Reader Correction Dossier for Hecke Theta Normalizations](neukirch/07-theta-normalization-reader-corrections.md) — Neukirch VII §7; `NA0506`. Expose a substantive cycle of convention tracking, counterexample construction, formula repair, and later source comparison.
- [From One-Dimensional Nonvanishing to Chebotarev Density](neukirch/07-chebotarev-two-routes.md) — Neukirch VII §13; `NA0535`. Compare cyclic-subgroup reduction with a character-expansion route, keeping the analytic input explicit.

## Qualifications, provenance, and use

The theta case is a **dated reader correction dossier**. Its original article explicitly reports that no matching formal erratum was obtained; publisher confirmation and a new audit are not claimed. The density case retains its coefficient caveat. The compactness case retains its topology caveats. The Hilbert-class-field motivation case retains existence as cited input. Historical completion and source coverage do not mean that every result in both books has an independent proof here.

Each page records a stable source ID, original date, underlying original-note hash, English-source hash, and exact scope. Printed pages and theorem numbers refer to the cited books. Textbook PDFs, scans, raw source archive, private automation prompts, and live runtime are not part of these cases. Excluded article IDs remain provenance references without broken local links. The public texts are editorial adaptations; their source hashes identify the preserved prior versions, not byte equality with the public adaptation.

The historical project’s optional **NM-HR-073** supplement remains unfinished within its qualified scope. `open-hard` is an internal reading-project label, not a claim that the problem is publicly unsolved. This selection does not pursue that branch or require readers to study it.

Choose your book, depth, pace, and learning aims. A local assistant can use these cases to suggest prerequisite reading, small diagnostic questions, and follow-up exercises without repeating the historical proof-audit campaign or restoring the original automation.
